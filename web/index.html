What am I looking at?

Brain: every one of the 176,571 neurons of an adult male Drosophila, at its real position in the FlyEM male CNS connectome (release v0.9) (~15 million connections). A leaky integrate-and-fire model was run on it with the giant fibre (DNp01, the escape neuron) stimulated for 15 ms. The full network needs far more memory than a web page can use, so this is a recording of that native run, replayed frame by frame with the same rendering as the desktop viewer. Activity reaches the ventral nerve cord about 7 ms after the brain lights up: roughly four synaptic hops.

Body: this one is live. The fly's skeleton and physics (33 rigid bodies, 34 joints, contact and friction) are the original C++ engine compiled to WebAssembly, running in your browser one millisecond at a time. Its muscles are driven by the motor-neuron activity recorded from the same giant-fibre run, so the jump you see emerges from the connectome rather than from an animation. It runs in slow motion because the solver is expensive: even natively, one millisecond of fly time costs tens of milliseconds of computation.

A caveat worth knowing: the takeoff is very sensitive to floating-point rounding. With bit-identical muscle input, the same C++ gives a peak of 2.9 mm in an optimised native build, 4.5 mm in a strict native build and 7.4 mm here. The nervous system and the muscle drive are deterministic; the difference arises in the contact and joint solver during the jump, which makes it an honest open problem of this model rather than a property of the fly.
