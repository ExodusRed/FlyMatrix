What am I looking at?

Brain: every one of the 176,571 neurons of an adult male Drosophila, at its real position in the FlyEM male CNS connectome (release v0.9) (~15 million connections). A leaky integrate-and-fire model was run on it with the giant fibre (DNp01, the escape neuron) stimulated for 15 ms. The full network needs far more memory than a web page can use, so this is a recording of that native run, replayed frame by frame with the same rendering as the desktop viewer. Activity reaches the ventral nerve cord about 7 ms after the brain lights up: roughly four synaptic hops.

Body: this one is live. The fly's skeleton and physics (33 rigid bodies, 34 joints, contact and friction) are the original C++ engine compiled to WebAssembly, running in your browser one millisecond at a time. Its muscles are driven by the motor-neuron activity recorded from the same giant-fibre run, so the jump you see emerges from the connectome rather than from an animation. Both modes run in slow motion because the solver is expensive: one millisecond of fly time costs a few milliseconds of computation.

Walk (the default) is a kinematic fly: no forces, just natural-looking movement that runs in real time. Its body is the flybody model by Google DeepMind and HHMI Janelia: measured skeleton, joint axes and ranges, and 3D meshes of every body part, here simplified to a tenth of their triangles. How it walks is FlyMatrix's own controller. Each leg has its own swing/stance controller; feet stay planted while the body moves over them; a leg lifts when its foot falls behind its reach, and local rules between neighbouring legs (don't lift while a neighbour swings, lift sooner after a neighbour lands) let the gait emerge: a tripod when fast, more legs on the ground when slow, as in real flies. Inverse kinematics moves all seven joints of each leg, including a three-axis coxa, the way the leg pairs of real flies do: front and hind legs mostly bend at the femur-tibia joint, middle legs rotate the coxa and femur. These ideas are inspired by published work on insect walking (Walknet; DeAngelis et al. 2019; Karashchuk et al. 2021), not copied from it.

Physics is the earlier prototype of a physical walker: the real physics body driven by a pattern generator. It is rougher, and is the starting point for a version that uses the same leg controllers with real forces. Neither walking rhythm comes from the connectome: in this model the wiring alone does not produce a walking rhythm, which remains an open research question.

A caveat worth knowing: the takeoff is very sensitive to floating-point rounding. With bit-identical muscle input, the same C++ gives a peak of 2.1 mm in an optimised native build, 4.5 mm in a strict native build and 2.7 mm here. The nervous system and the muscle drive are deterministic; the difference arises in the contact and joint solver during the jump, which makes it an honest open problem of this model rather than a property of the fly.
